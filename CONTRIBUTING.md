# Forking and proposing improvements

The Frontier Constitution is a reusable personal-AI governance charter. GitHub provides a public venue for distribution, discussion and proposed improvements. Patrick Yaeger's private knowledge system remains his canonical governing source.

## Your independent adaptation

Use the repository's **Fork** action or copy the [v1.0 baseline](https://github.com/pyaeger/frontier-constitution/tree/v1.0). You may adapt the constitutional text under [CC BY-SA 4.0](LICENSE) without Patrick's approval. His owner-approval rule governs his canonical Constitution, not your independently licensed adaptation.

Give the adaptation its own identity and owner. Retain required attribution and notices, indicate changes, and comply with ShareAlike when sharing adapted material. The [README attribution example](README.md#attribution-example) provides a starting point. Do not imply Patrick's endorsement. The license covers the constitutional text only; it does not grant rights to branding, website code, artwork, private material or other excluded works.

## A proposal for Patrick's Constitution

1. Read the current text and [change history](CHANGELOG.md).
2. Open an issue for discussion, or make a branch in your fork, edit the text and open a pull request with the proposed diff.
3. Identify the article or passage, the problem, your rationale and downstream implications. Distinguish observation, evidence, interpretation and uncertainty. Preserve material dissent.
4. Explain how the proposal affects human authority, delegation, privacy, auditability, reversibility and model or vendor dependence where relevant.
5. Keep private records, credentials, precise network details and nonpublic employer information out of issues and pull requests. Use public or synthetic examples.

A pull request is a proposal. **Only Patrick's explicit, recorded approval can amend his canonical Constitution.** Review, consensus and a merged documentation change are not substitutes for that decision.

For an approved constitutional amendment, Patrick's governing record must state the decision, date, purpose, new version and downstream implications. The canonical source is updated under his authority, then verified publication derivatives are released. This repository does not automatically write to the canonical source or deploy runtime changes.

## Preserve the baseline

- Do not move or overwrite `v1.0`, backdate a release or replace the historical public baseline with revised text.
- Publish substantive or literary changes as separately versioned releases after the required canonical approval.
- Keep the original v1.0 release and historical website links available as future versions develop.
- Documentation corrections may clarify publication mechanics without changing constitutional text or version. Make that distinction explicit in the commit and change history.

## Evidence and verification

Compare proposed public text with the authorized canonical version before publication. Preserve source/version provenance and inspect the complete outgoing diff. Check Markdown rendering, license scope, attribution, links and release availability.

Treat governing requirements and verified implementation separately. A charter, source-code change, successful build or passing monitor does not alone establish comprehensive runtime enforcement. Report the scope and date of evidence, material limitations and unresolved questions.
